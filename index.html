GOPHER · Patent Pending

A Tunneling Vehicle
That Anchors Itself.

GOPHER is a small-diameter, self-propelled, tethered unmanned underground vehicle. Two sets of gripper shoes take turns anchoring it against the wall of its own bore, so one set is gripped at all times, and its own spoil buries the tether behind it.

Overview

Ground-Operated Platform For Hazardous Excavation, Remote.

Mines, tunnels, utility corridors and collapsed structures can become inaccessible through roof falls, flooding, fire, hazardous atmosphere or structural failure. Before anyone can enter safely, operators may need to establish communications, assess conditions, and deliver water, medical supplies or sensors. GOPHER is a proposed remotely operated micro-boring platform for creating that access.

01

Walks Out Of Its Launch Tube

Many conventional microtunneling systems require launch shafts, thrust-reaction frames, jacking pits and installed pipe strings. GOPHER launches from a tube, walking out of it under its own dual-anchor cycle with no separate pusher.

02

Reacts Its Own Thrust

Both anchor sets react thrust against the bore wall, so cutterhead thrust does not depend on the vehicle's weight.

03

Keeps Its Supply Line

Discharged spoil fills the bore behind the vehicle and buries the tether, which remains the operative supply line.

How It Moves

One Set Always Grips.

Each body carries an independently actuated set of radially extensible gripper shoes. The sets alternate as anchors while linear actuators spanning the articulation extend and retract, so the vehicle advances like an inchworm.

  1. 7A

    Anchor

    The rear gripper set is engaged and the actuators are fully retracted.

  2. 7B

    Push And Bore

    The actuators extend, driving the front body forward while the cutting head bores.

  3. 7C

    Hand Over

    The front gripper set engages and the cutting head stops.

  4. 7D

    Draw Up

    The rear gripper set releases and the actuators retract, drawing the rear body forward.

  5. 7E

    Reset

    The rear gripper set re-engages and the front set releases, returning the vehicle to the first state.

Architecture

Two Bodies, One Joint, One Spoil Path.

A rear body and a front body are joined by an articulation that permits angular deflection. The spoil conduit, coolant, water and wiring all cross that joint, and the spoil path stays sealed through the full range of deflection and extension.

01

Dual-Anchor Propulsion

The rear set anchors while the actuators extend and drive the front body and cutterhead forward. The front set anchors while they retract and draw the rear body forward. Each shoe rides on guided stems whose bores in web rings carry the shoe's axial anchor load.

02

Guide-Ball Articulation

A guide ball is carried on a sleeve that stays coaxial with the rear body. The ball is seated in the front body, so the front body deflects about it. Torque-reacting pins carried by the ball engage slots in the front body seat.

03

Through-Articulation Spoil Transport

A first auger, turning with the cutterhead, lifts crushed material through a hollow head shaft. A second, independently driven auger carries it the length of the vehicle to a discharge port at the tail. The conduit crosses the joint through a telescoping splined shaft with a universal joint at the center of the guide ball.

04

Self-Burying Tether

The tether pays out from the rear body. Spoil discharged at the tail fills the bore behind the vehicle and buries the tether, which remains a supply line after the vehicle stops.

05

Service Crossing

Coolant, water and wiring cross the articulation on a rigid fluid rail and a rigid wire conduit that slide in bushings in the rear body. Fixed fluid stems telescope inside the rail, and a rolling wire loop feeds the conduit, so fluid and electrical services cross the articulation through its full stroke and deflection without flexible hose.

06

Payload Module

The forward face of the vehicle behind the cutterhead is sealed. Supplies travel in a payload module in two cassettes, both loaded before launch.

07

Cutterhead

A pilot bit leads the face. The face carries ten conical picks on five tracks, gauge rollers and muck apertures, with a crusher ring and crushing cone behind it. The head auger lifts crushed material through a hollow head shaft.

08

Closed Coolant Circuit

A closed circuit of pumps, a cold plate, motor jackets and a full-length jacket.

Missions

Reach The Space, Then Stay Connected To It.

When people are trapped behind a roof fall, responders may drill from the surface, steer a small bore from a nearby shaft, or dig by hand through unstable rubble. Where there is no access from above, hand excavation from an existing drift is slow and hazardous. GOPHER is intended to address that gap, boring from a launch drift.

Rescue

Search-And-Rescue Support

Breakthrough is made through the side rib of the occupied drift, crossing it at 45 degrees or more. The approach is governed by a pilot bit that opens its hole before the face does, a hold state triggered by a drop in head thrust, and interlocks that permit head rotation and actuator motion only in the correct grip state of each shoe set.

Access

Inspection And Infrastructure

Hazardous environment inspection, utility and infrastructure assessment, geological or environmental sampling, sensor emplacement and communications extension.

Off-World

Extraterrestrial Operations

In an extraterrestrial environment the vehicle may bore in regolith, ice-cemented regolith or rock to reach a lava tube, sample subsurface ice or emplace instruments. Cutterhead thrust does not depend on the vehicle's weight, which enables operation at any inclination in low gravity. In a cable-laying variant, the vehicle leaves a power or data cable in the bore and the discharged spoil buries it.
